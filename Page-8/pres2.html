The 2nd President: Manuel L. Quezon

Manuel L. Quezon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (summarized)

Manuel Luis Quezon y Molina was a Filipino statesman who served as president of the Commonwealth of the Philippines from 1935 until his death in 1944. He is regarded as the second president of the Philippines and the first elected by a national vote.

Early life and career

Quezon served in the Philippine-American War and became a lawyer. He was Resident Commissioner to the United States House of Representatives (1909-1916) and Senate President of the Philippines (1916-1935), where he led the campaign for independence.

Presidency

Elected in 1935 under the Tydings-McDuffie Act, Quezon led the Commonwealth's transition toward independence. His administration promoted social justice, women's suffrage (1937) and a national language based on Tagalog. After the Japanese invasion in 1941 he led the government-in-exile in the United States.

Death and legacy

Quezon died of tuberculosis on August 1, 1944. Quezon City and Quezon Province are named in his honor.
