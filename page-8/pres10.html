The 10th President: Ferdinand Marcos

Ferdinand Marcos

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (summarized)

Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos Sr. was a Filipino lawyer and politician who served as the tenth president of the Philippines, from 1965 to 1986. His 21-year rule, including nine years of martial law, was marked by authoritarianism, widespread human rights abuses and corruption.

Early life and career

A lawyer from Ilocos Norte, Marcos served in the House of Representatives (1949-1959) and the Senate (1959-1965), becoming Senate President in 1963. He won the presidency in 1965 and in 1969 became the first president to be re-elected.

Presidency and martial law

On September 21, 1972, Marcos declared martial law, closing Congress and jailing opponents. He ruled under the 1973 Constitution until martial law was lifted in 1981. His regime built major infrastructure but accumulated large foreign debt, and is documented to have committed thousands of killings, torture and arbitrary detentions, and to have plundered billions of dollars.

Downfall and death

After the assassination of Benigno Aquino Jr. in 1983, opposition grew. A disputed snap election in February 1986 triggered the People Power Revolution, and Marcos fled to Hawaii on February 25, 1986. He died in exile on September 28, 1989.
